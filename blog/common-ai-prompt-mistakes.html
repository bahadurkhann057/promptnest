Common Mistakes People Make When Writing AI Prompts

Published August 2026

Most disappointing AI results trace back to a handful of repeatable prompt mistakes. Here are the ones that come up most often, and how to fix each one.

1. Being too vague

"A landscape" or "help me write something" gives the model almost nothing to work with, so it defaults to the most generic interpretation possible. Add a subject, a style or tone, and ideally a purpose.

2. Cramming contradictory ideas into one prompt

Asking for "minimalist and highly detailed" or "brief but comprehensive" sends mixed signals. Pick one direction per prompt — you can always generate a second version taking the other approach.

3. Forgetting to specify format

For chat prompts especially, not stating whether you want a list, a table, a short paragraph, or a long explanation means the model has to guess — and it often guesses wrong for what you actually needed.

4. Writing a full sentence when a phrase list would work better

This applies mainly to image prompts. Tools like Midjourney parse comma-separated descriptive phrases more effectively than grammatically complete sentences — "a cat, sitting on a windowsill, soft morning light" tends to outperform "There is a cat that is sitting on a windowsill in the soft morning light."

5. Giving up after one bad result

A weak first output usually isn't a dead end — it's information. Look at what went wrong, adjust one or two specific elements, and try again rather than abandoning the idea entirely.

6. Not giving the AI a role for chat tasks

Skipping a role instruction ("Act as a...") means the model answers in a generic, average tone instead of the specific expertise or voice that would actually suit your task.

7. Overloading a single prompt with too many tasks

Asking for a blog post, an SEO title, a meta description, and social captions all in one prompt usually produces a mediocre version of everything. Breaking it into separate, focused prompts gets better results per task.

8. Ignoring length control

Not specifying roughly how long you want a response means you might get three sentences when you wanted a full page, or vice versa. A simple word or sentence count instruction fixes this immediately.

Avoiding these mistakes is mostly about being specific rather than assuming the AI will infer what you meant.
