10 Tips for Writing Better Midjourney Prompts

Published August 2026

A weak prompt gives Midjourney too little to work with, so it fills in the gaps with generic choices. A strong prompt removes that guesswork. Here are ten practical ways to get sharper, more intentional results.

1. Start with the subject, then build outward

Lead with what the image is actually of, then layer on style, lighting, and composition — in that order. Midjourney tends to weight earlier words more heavily.

2. Name a specific art style, not a vague one

"Digital art" is vague. "Ukiyo-e woodblock print" or "1980s airbrush illustration" gives the model something concrete to anchor to.

3. Describe the lighting

Lighting does more to set mood than almost anything else. Try phrases like golden hour lighting, dramatic chiaroscuro, or soft overcast light.

4. Specify the camera angle or composition

Terms like low-angle shot, wide establishing shot, or close-up framing push the model toward a specific visual structure instead of a default centered pose.

5. Use comma-separated phrases, not full sentences

Midjourney responds better to a list of descriptive phrases separated by commas than to a grammatically complete sentence — it's parsing for concepts, not syntax.

6. Add a quality/detail modifier

Phrases like highly detailed, sharp focus, or 8k resolution nudge the output toward more refined rendering.

7. Reference a mood, not just a subject

Words like eerie, serene, triumphant, or melancholic shape color grading and atmosphere even when nothing in the scene explicitly shows emotion.

8. Keep contradictory ideas out of one prompt

"Minimalist" and "highly detailed" pull in opposite directions. Pick a lane — you can always generate a second variation with the other approach.

9. Iterate instead of aiming for a perfect first prompt

Treat your first generation as a draft. Look at what came out, then adjust one or two elements rather than rewriting the whole prompt from scratch.

10. Save prompts that work

When a combination of style + lighting + composition produces a result you like, keep a note of it — it'll usually transfer well to other subjects too.
